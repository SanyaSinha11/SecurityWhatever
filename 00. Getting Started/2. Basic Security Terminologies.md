# Initial Security Terms
In cybersecurity, a **threat** is the potential danger to your systems, a **vulnerability** is the weakness that can be exploited, an **exploit** is the specific method or tool used to abuse that weakness, and an **attack** is the broader, active execution of that exploit. 

### Threat
- **What it is:** 
  Any potential event, person, or circumstance—such as malware, a hacker, or a natural disaster—that has the potential to harm an organization or disrupt systems.
- **Analogy:** 
  A burglar walking down your street scoping out houses.
- **Key takeaway:** It is the _danger itself_.

### Vulnerability
- **What it is:** 
  A flaw, bug, or misconfiguration in a system's design or software that leaves it open to compromise.
- **Analogy:** 
  Leaving a window unlocked on the ground floor.
- **Key takeaway:** It is the _system's weakness_.

### Exploit 
- **What it is:** 
  A specific piece of code, software, or technique designed to take advantage of a specific vulnerability.
- **Analogy:** 
  Using a crowbar and a rock to force the unlocked window open and climb inside.
- **Key takeaway:** It is the _method or tool_ used to breach the weakness.

### Attack
- **What it is:** 
  The actual execution of hostile intent; the broader action of using exploits to compromise a target, steal data, or disrupt operations.
- **Analogy:** 
  The entire process of the burglar breaking into your house, stealing your valuables, and vandalizing the property.
- **Key takeaway:** It is the _active execution_ of the malicious activity.

### Threat Vector
- Path that a threat actor uses to execute a data exfiltration, service disruption, or disinformation attack.
- Sophisticated threat actors will make use of multiple vectors.
- Highly capable threat actors will be able to develop novel vectors.
  
	- The terms **"threat vector"** and **"attack vector"** are often taken to mean the same thing. Some sources distinguish the use of threat vector to refer to analysis of the potential attack surface and attack vector to analyze an exploit that has been successfully executed.

# Some Other Terms
### Cryptanalysis
The art of cracking cryptographic systems.

### Typosquatting
It means that the threat actor registers a domain name very similar to a real one, such as `exannple.com`, hoping that users will not notice the difference and assume they are browsing a trusted site or receiving email from a known source. These are also referred to as _cousin, lookalike,_ or _dopp__elganger_ domains.

 Involves fake websites with similar URLs, not deceptive emails.

### Watering Hole Attack
A watering hole attack relies on a group of targets that use an unsecure third-party website. For example, staff running an international e-commerce site might use a local pizza delivery firm. A threat actor might discover this fact through social engineering or other reconnaissance of the target. An attacker can compromise the pizza delivery firm's website so that it runs exploit code on visitors. They may be able to infect the computers of the e-commerce company's employees and penetrate the e-commerce company systems.

### Consensus Technique
The **consensus technique** is a social engineering tactic where an attacker tries to convince someone to trust them by claiming that _“everyone else is doing it”_ or _“others have already approved this.”_ It plays on human psychology—people tend to follow what they believe is the majority or accepted behavior.



**Example:**  
An attacker emails an employee saying, _“All your team members have already submitted their login details for verification—please do the same to avoid account suspension.”_ The employee may feel pressured to comply because it seems like a normal and widely accepted action, even though it’s actually a scam.

# Note:

There are two types of Models in an organization:
1. Discretionary Model
2. Mandatory Model
In a discretionary model, the object owner can allocate rights. 
In a mandatory model, rights are predetermined by system-enforced rules and cannot be changed by any user within the system.
